Not All Gambling Is Equal

Slots exist for one reason.

They are engineered to keep you playing. House edge on most slots runs between 5% and 15%. That means for every $100 you put in, the math takes $5 to $15 before you even start.

Roulette looks flashy. American roulette has a 5.26% house edge. European roulette drops it to 2.7%. Neither is smart compared to what's available.

Parlays in sports betting feel exciting. They are the sportsbook's favorite product for a reason — each leg compounds the house edge. A four-team parlay at standard juice gives the book roughly a 30% edge before any of your picks matter.

These are not your best options if you care about the math.

The Three Games Worth Playing

1. Blackjack

The lowest house edge of any table game when played with basic strategy. Usually under 0.5%.

That number only holds if you play correctly. Learn basic strategy before you sit down — it is a one-page chart and it matters.

The rules that separate disciplined players from everyone else:

→Never take side bets. They add 5–15% house edge on top.
→Never chase losses. The math does not care about your bad run.
→Set a loss limit before you sit. Walk when you hit it.
→Do not mix decisions with alcohol. This is obvious. Most people ignore it anyway.

If you do not know basic strategy you are donating at a slightly slower rate than slots.

2. Baccarat

Simpler than blackjack. One real decision: banker or player.

Always bet banker. House edge on banker is 1.06%. House edge on player is 1.24%. Ignore tie bets — 14.4% edge, they exist purely to extract money faster.

Good for players who want fewer decisions and less mental overhead. The bankroll rules are identical to blackjack — fixed amount before you sit, walk at your limit, no chasing.

3. Poker

The only casino game where you play against other people instead of the house.

The house takes a small percentage of each pot as rake. The rest is skill and patience versus other players.

If you are disciplined and can read situations you have a real edge over recreational players who play emotionally.

If you tilt easily or play impulsively start with blackjack first. Poker rewards patience above everything.

4. Straight Sports Bets

A moneyline or spread bet is one decision with transparent odds. The house edge comes from the vig — usually 4–5% on standard -110 lines.

That is far better than a parlay, which multiplies the vig on every leg.

Straight bets are where discipline and information give you the best real shot at a positive expected value over time.

The Rules That Actually Protect Your Bankroll

This part is not exciting. It is the difference between players who last and players who donate.

Six rules every disciplined player follows:

1. Set a fixed bankroll before every session. When it is gone the session is over. No exceptions.

2. Define a win target. If you are up 40% of your session bankroll — walk. Most players give it all back.

3. Never chase losses. You are not owed a comeback. The math does not remember your bad run.

4. Avoid side bets in every game. They are designed to look appealing and they consistently extract money.

5. Avoid martingale systems. They feel logical and they destroy bankrolls. If this needs explaining look up the table limit problem.
